Bloom Clipper is $29, once. Not $29/month. Not $29 to start with usage caps after. Twenty-nine dollars, and it's yours on however many of your own machines you want to run it on.

## The trade-off nobody mentions: your footage leaves your computer

Every cloud-based auto-clipper needs your source video on its servers to process it. For a lot of creators that's a non-issue. For anyone clipping client interviews under NDA, unreleased music, pre-broadcast news footage, or a wedding video someone trusted you with, it's a real liability question that rarely gets asked before the upload button gets clicked.

Bloom Clipper never uploads anything. The source file stays on your disk, ffmpeg renders locally, and the app works identically with your network disconnected. There's no server to breach, no terms-of-service clause to worry about, no "how long do you retain my footage" question to email support about.

## What you lose without the AI picking clips for you

To be fair to the algorithm-first tools: if you genuinely don't know which 30 seconds of a 90-minute recording will land, an auto-clipper's "virality score" is a real head start. Bloom Clipper doesn't do that guessing for you — it assumes you already know your best moment and gives you a fast, precise way to cut and frame it.

In practice, most regular creators — podcasters, YouTubers, coaches — already know their best moment when they hear it back. The bottleneck isn't finding it. It's the twenty minutes of manual cropping and re-exporting that follows. That's the twenty minutes Bloom Clipper gets back.

## Where the manual crop box actually wins

Auto-reframe tools track faces and center on them. That works for a single speaker sitting still. It falls apart the moment there are two people, a slide on screen, a whiteboard, or product in frame — the algorithm has to guess what matters, and it guesses wrong often enough to be annoying.

Bloom Clipper's Full 9:16 mode gives you one draggable, zoomable crop box per cut. You decide what's in frame, and you can zoom in tight on exactly the moment that needs it — a reaction, a product, a chart — without the algorithm second-guessing you mid-render.

## The interview case: Half 9:16

This is the feature most auto-clippers handle worst. A two-person interview needs both faces visible without a jarring whip-pan between them every time someone else talks. Bloom Clipper's Half 9:16 mode is built specifically for this: two aspect-locked half-height boxes, one per speaker, stacked automatically into a single 1080x1920 frame. No pan, no guessing who's talking — both faces, always.
