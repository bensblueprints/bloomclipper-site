If you've looked up Opus Clip pricing recently, you've probably noticed something: the official pricing page tells you less than it used to. You get a monthly dollar figure per plan, and that's largely it — no annual price breakdown, no clear statement of how many minutes or credits each tier actually includes. You find those details, when you find them at all, scattered across help-center articles and third-party roundups that frequently disagree with each other.

That opacity is itself the story. Before you commit to a recurring tool, it's worth being precise about what's actually confirmed on Opus Clip's own site today, versus what's secondhand and disputed — and what that means for the real cost of clipping over a year, not just the headline number.

## What's confirmed directly from Opus Clip's pricing page

As of this writing, Opus Clip's pricing page (opus.pro/pricing) lists four tiers:

- **Free** — $0/month. Watermark-free export is not included; animated caption templates are explicitly marked "with watermark."
- **Starter** — $15/month. Watermark-free export is included.
- **Pro** — $29/month. Watermark-free export is included. The page's Zapier integration notes reference "up to 300 credits/mo" for this tier.
- **Business** — custom, quote-based pricing.

Notice what's missing from that list: there's no annual price shown for any paid tier, and no plan-by-plan statement of exactly how many processing minutes or credits you get. That's a meaningful change from how subscription pricing pages used to work — a clear monthly-vs-annual table with specific usage caps spelled out side by side. Now you're expected to sign up, or dig through secondary sources, to find out what a plan actually gets you.

## Why that matters more than it sounds like

A handful of third-party pricing trackers have tried to fill in the gaps, and they don't agree with each other. Some say Starter includes roughly 150 credits a month and Pro roughly 300. Others describe annual billing working out to something in the neighborhood of $174–$233 a year for Pro, depending on which snapshot of the pricing page they captured and when. At least one tracker claims the credit allotment and rollover rules for Pro changed as recently as October 2026; others who checked around the same window describe a different structure entirely.

The honest takeaway isn't "here's the exact number" — it's that the number moves, and it moves in ways that aren't always clearly announced. If you're budgeting for a subscription tool as a fixed, predictable line item, that's worth knowing going in. Check opus.pro/pricing yourself at the moment you're about to buy, because whatever figure you read in any article — including this one — may not be the figure you're quoted at checkout.

## Credits are the unit, and they're not 1:1 with "a clip"

Across the sources that have actually dug into Opus Clip's credit documentation, one point comes up consistently: credits are spent based on the length of the **source video you process**, not per clip you export. Feed in a 90-minute podcast episode and you're drawing down credits against that full 90 minutes, regardless of whether you end up exporting 3 clips or 30 from it. That's a different cost model than "pay per clip," and it changes the math depending on how you work — someone who processes long raw footage to pull just a couple of clips pays more per clip, in credits, than someone who processes efficiently and exports in bulk.

Credit expiry adds another wrinkle: multiple sources describe monthly-plan credits expiring within roughly 60 days if unused, while annual-plan credits are described as staying valid longer. If you have a slow month — you're traveling, sick, between seasons of a show — credits you already paid for can quietly evaporate before you use them. That's a cost that never shows up in the sticker price.

## A concrete example: what this looks like for a weekly podcast

Say you run a weekly podcast and want 4-6 clips from each episode. A typical episode runs 60-90 minutes, and under the "credits spent on source minutes processed" model described above, that means every single episode draws down close to a full hour-and-a-half of credits — whether you pull 4 clips from it or 14. Over a year of weekly episodes, that's roughly 50 processing passes, each consuming a large chunk of whatever monthly allotment you're on.

If your allotment runs out mid-month — easy to do if you also reprocess an episode after a bad take, or batch-process a backlog of older recordings — you're either waiting for the next cycle, paying for a top-up, or stepping up a tier you don't otherwise need for your day-to-day volume. None of that shows up when you glance at "$29/month" on the pricing page. It shows up the first time you hit a cap mid-project, with a deadline the same day.

This is the part that's easy to miss when comparing tools by sticker price alone: the $29 or $15 figure is the floor, not the ceiling, of what a credit-metered tool can cost you in a given month. A one-time tool doesn't have a floor-vs-ceiling problem in the first place — there's one number, and it's the same number every month, including $0 in the months you don't clip anything at all.

## Running the actual yearly math

Set the disputed details aside and just use the one number that's unambiguous on Opus Clip's own page: Pro is $29/month if you pay monthly. That's $348 a year, full stop, before any annual discount, before credit top-ups if you run over your allotment, and before adding a second seat if anyone else on your team needs their own login. Even the more optimistic annual-billing estimates floating around third-party trackers put Pro in the $174–$233/year range — real savings over paying monthly, but still a bill that renews every single year, indefinitely, for as long as you keep clipping.
